# snhu-cs255-portfolio
Portfolio for CS255 – System Analysis and Design

# Reflection
## Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
DriverPass was a project built around a small driving instruction company that wanted a full online system to support their training services. The client needed a platform where students could schedule driving lessons, take practice tests, and track their progress. They also needed tools for instructors, the secretary, and the admin to manage accounts, schedules, and training packages. The goal was to design a system that made everything easier and more organized for both students and staff.

## What did you do particularly well?
I think I did a good job translating the client’s goals into a clear system structure. I made sure the requirements, diagrams, and workflows all lined up with what DriverPass actually needed, not just what would look good on paper. I also kept the design consistent across the different documents, so the use case diagram, activity diagram, and requirements all supported each other instead of feeling disconnected. That made the final design feel organized and intentional.

## If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
If I were to revise something, I would probably refine the use case diagram. It worked, but I could make it cleaner by tightening up the layout and making the relationships between user groups and actions a little more visually balanced. I’d also spend more time making sure the diagram is easier to read at a glance.

## How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
I interpreted the user’s needs by focusing on what DriverPass actually does day to day. Students need simple scheduling and progress tracking. Instructors need a clear schedule and a way to record lesson details. Staff need tools to manage accounts and packages. I made sure each part of the design supported those real tasks. Considering user needs is important because a system only works if it fits the people who use it. If the design doesn’t match their workflow, it becomes frustrating instead of helpful.

## How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
I approach software design by breaking the problem down into smaller pieces and figuring out what each user needs to accomplish. Diagrams help me visualize how everything connects, and writing out requirements keeps the project grounded. In the future, I’d keep using structured techniques like use case analysis, activity diagrams, and clear requirement lists. They make it easier to stay organized and build a system that actually solves the client’s problems.
